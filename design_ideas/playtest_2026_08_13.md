# Playtest — 2026-08-13

First **coast-to-coast** session, host plus a distant client. Reported verbatim
below, with what is already known about each. Nothing here is fixed by this
document; items that already have a plan say where it is.

---

## 1. More enemy variety is needed

Rushers are the only enemy in the game, and `hazards.md` already has the roster
queued behind them: barrels (**~free** — a plinko ball that starts at rest, and
the first hazard players aim at *each other*), angry rocks, spikes, sand,
saw-blades, spiders.

**Barrels are the cheapest real answer** and the doc already argues it: the ball
sim, the ricochet, tumble-on-contact and the 4° pitch all exist. Worth taking
before anything with pathfinding in it.

## 2. Look at lag prediction

Audited in full: **`design_ideas/netcode_assessment.md`**, with the milestone in
`implementation_plans/m13_netcode.md`.

The short version: the local avatar is predicted and reconciled properly, and
nothing else is. There is no interpolation anywhere, the client never reads the
host tick the snapshot already carries, `SHOVE` is not predicted so the game's
signature verb has a full round trip of dead air, and a busy snapshot is 2.5×
ENet's MTU sent 60 times a second.

## 3. The downed indicator is hard to see

Asked for: **flashing, and a dorito at the screen edge when the player is
offscreen.**

Some of this exists. The head bar has four states and hanging/downed is red on
black (and now blue while somebody is hauling). What it does not have is any
**flash**, and there is **no offscreen indicator at all** — `m9_hud.md` §"One
addition D5 does not specify" already flagged that the fixed-yaw camera plus a
40 m leash means a teammate can be off the edge of the screen, and the HUD model
already computes `bearing` and `distance` per friend for exactly this. So the
data is there and nothing draws it.

Two separate pieces of work: a flash on the world bar, and an edge marker driven
off the bearing the model already has.

## 4. Ramps are still buggy, and **worse the farther up the map you go**

> "I both fell through the gap at the bottom and got stuck mid ramp."

The gap at the bottom was found, measured and fixed today — every ramp in the
game was a paper edge over a `DECK_THICKNESS`-deep void (see the `test_ramp_solid`
commit). That fix was not in the build being played.

**"Worse the farther up you go" is new information and the most useful line in
this report**, because it points at something the bottom-gap fix does not
address. It says the defect is a function of *segment offset*, not of ramp shape.
One concrete candidate found by reading, not yet confirmed:

- **`SegmentBuilder._height_behind` cannot see the previous segment.** At `z == 0`
  it returns the cell's *own* height, so a ramp authored on the **first row of a
  segment** has `bottom_h == top_h`, no rise, and `_wedge_mesh` silently falls
  back to a **flat slab**. No segment authors one today, so this is latent rather
  than the reported bug — but it is the same class, and the streaming assembler
  is the thing that varies with distance up the map.

To measure: sweep the solidity probe from `test_ramp_solid` across a run of
**many streamed segments** rather than one, and see whether thickness, surface
continuity or slope degrade with `z_offset` / `h_offset`.

## 5. Pillars catch you going around them, and should be 20% narrower

Two asks in one line, and they may be the same fix. A pillar is a
`CylinderShape3D` of radius 1.0 in a 2 m cell — it fills its cell exactly, so two
diagonally adjacent pillars touch at a point and a 0.4 m player cannot pass. 20%
narrower is radius 0.8, which opens a 0.4 m diagonal gap.

Worth checking whether "gets stuck going around" is that pinch, the cylinder-vs-
cylinder tangent case, or the same flat-bottomed-body-on-a-seam problem
`CLAUDE.md` already documents twice.

## 6. Plinko balls hit you from a distance, going past

`_resolve_ball_hits` uses `BALL_RADIUS + 0.5` (**1.1 m**) plus `HALF_HEIGHT`
(0.9), so the test radius is **2.0 m** measured centre to centre — against a ball
that is 0.6 m and a body that is 0.4 m, whose real contact distance is 1.0 m.
**The hit radius is twice the geometry.**

The direction test is only a sign check (`velocity.dot(toward) > 0`), which is
true of a ball passing at ninety degrees, so a ball sailing *past* at 2 m counts
as coming at you. Both halves are named in the code's own comments as deliberate
simplifications; this report is the evidence that the margin is too generous.

## Also asked for: a networked debug console

> "A debugging menu that lets any player tweak parameters and have it reflected
> for all players. Assume a large config, not a small number of bespoke knobs.
> The first setting should be show hitboxes."

Planned in `implementation_plans/m14_debug_console.md`. `DebugSettings` already
exists as exactly the right shape — a registry of options with environment
overrides — so the work is replication, a menu that builds itself from the
registry, and the first real entry.

This is the item most likely to pay for itself immediately: every other line in
this report is a number somebody wants to try a different value for, and hitbox
visualisation answers 5 and 6 directly.
