# Playtest — 2026-08-14

The session after M15 landed: two enemies that shoot, three specials, the debug
console, delta encoding and dash prediction. Reported verbatim below, with what is
already known about each. **Nothing here is fixed by this document**; items that
already have a plan say where it is.

Two items are repeats from 2026-08-13 and are marked **again** — that is the most
important signal in the list, because it means the previous round's answer either
did not land or did not address the thing being reported.

---

## 1. Half speed seemed good for the rusher — make it the new default

**The clearest result of the day, and the cheapest to act on.** `rusher_speed_pct`
was added to the debug console for exactly this question and it answered it in one
session, which is the argument for that whole milestone in one line.

The knob is a **percentage of `RUSHER_SPEED`**, deliberately, so that a playtest
answers "is the shipped value right" rather than inventing a second number nobody
can trace. The result should therefore be spent on the constant, not on the
default of the knob:

- `SimConfig.RUSHER_SPEED` halves.
- `rusher_speed_pct` goes back to defaulting to 100, still meaning "the shipped
  value", so the next session starts from the new baseline rather than from a
  knob that is quietly at 50.

**Check what else reads it before halving.** The rusher's whole design is that it
is answered by *moving*, so its speed is in a relationship with `WALK_SPEED` (6)
and with the dash. At half speed a rusher is slower than a walking player, which
changes it from "you must react" to "you can simply leave" — that may be exactly
what made it feel good, or it may make the mound telegraph pointless. Worth one
deliberate look at `test_rusher`'s assertions, which encode the current
relationship.

## 2. Collisions trip up prediction — players touching each other glitches

**The most interesting technical finding of the session, and it has a specific
cause.**

The client predicts its own body and *nothing else*. Remote players are not
simulated on a client at all — they are placed wherever the last snapshot said,
with no interpolation (see `netcode_assessment.md`). Meanwhile the player's own
collision mask includes other players, so:

> Predicting your own body requires knowing where everyone else's body is, and
> that is exactly the thing a client does not have accurately.

The host resolves the local player against the *host's* idea of where the other
player is. The client resolves the same step against a position that is at best
one snapshot stale and at worst several frames old. Two different collision
resolutions, so the reconcile finds a mismatch, corrects, and the correction is
the glitch being reported. Touching another player is the one situation where
prediction cannot be right on its own.

**This is the strongest argument yet for M13's entity interpolation**, which was
deferred pending a playtest — and this is that playtest reporting back. Smoothing
remote players is not only a smoothness win; it makes the local player's
prediction *more correct*, because the body it is colliding against stops being a
stale point sample.

Three candidate answers, cheapest first, and they compose:

1. **Interpolate remote players** (M13, already planned). Attacks the cause.
2. **Watch `GameWorld.corrections`.** It already exists and it is the instrument
   for this: if it sits near zero alone and climbs while two players touch, the
   diagnosis above is confirmed rather than assumed. Do this before building
   anything.
3. **Soften player-vs-player contact.** If interpolation is not enough, the
   question is whether players need to collide with each other at all while the
   host is the only one that can settle it. That is a design decision, not a
   netcode one — shoving is a *verb*, and bumping is currently an accident.

## 3. Ramps still suck — **again**

Reported 2026-08-13, worked on, and reported again. Two real defects were found
and fixed since: the **paper edge** (a ramp cell had no floor under its first few
centimetres — 0.053 m of solid where the deck behind had 1.002 m) and the **seam**
between two ramp wedges that were merged along Z but not across X.

**Both were real and neither was the whole thing.** The honest reading is that the
remaining problem has not been identified yet, and re-reporting the same symptom
means the next round must not start by guessing again.

What the previous round proved, and what the next one should inherit:

- **Measure the structure, do not re-run the walk.** `test_ramp_traversal` walked
  a body up a ramp on every run for months while the first centimetres of every
  ramp in the game were a knife edge over a void. A body at `WALK_SPEED` crosses
  two centimetres of paper in a third of a tick.
- **Measure on a fixture with nothing else moving in it.** Two rounds were spent
  on rig artefacts — live shooters tumbling the probe, and a flat-bottomed
  cylinder placed centre-on-surface whose uphill rim is buried `radius *
  tan(slope)` deep.
- **"Sometimes" means something varies.** Ask what: lateral position found the
  seam.

**What is still not measured:** the ramp/deck *junction* at the top, the behaviour
of a body that arrives at an angle rather than straight up, and what
`floor_max_angle` does at the exact boundary between the gentle (27°) and steep
(45°) grades. `show_hitboxes` now draws bodies rather than the whole bridge, so it
is finally usable for looking at one — though note it deliberately no longer draws
the deck, and for a ramp the deck is the thing in question. **A ramp-only hitbox
mode may be worth adding before the next attempt.**

## 4. Effects on server authority? Don't play until the server says so?

A direct question, so here is a direct answer. The rule that resolves it is not
"host or client" but **who decided the thing being drawn**:

> **Predict what you initiated. Wait for what the world decided.**

An effect is *not retractable*. Once a player has seen an explosion, being told a
moment later that it did not happen is worse than having waited — there is no
"unflip". So the cost of guessing is asymmetric, and it decides each case:

| Effect | Who decides | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Muzzle flash, dash whoosh, shield going up | **your own input** | play it **immediately**, locally |
| Explosion, a hit landing, an enemy dying | **the host's simulation** | **wait** for the host |
| A body's position, a grenade's arc | host, but continuous | interpolate — never a yes/no |

This is already the split the code uses, and it is worth stating because it was
arrived at case by case rather than as a rule:

- **The blast effect is told, not inferred** (`_blast_seen`). A client could nearly
  work it out — a grenade stops being mentioned in the snapshot when it detonates
  — but that is *also* what happens when one falls off the bridge, and that
  deliberately is not a bang. Guessing would put an explosion over the void every
  time somebody missed. This is the asymmetry above, in one concrete case.
- **The shield wall is local**, because it is derived from your own trigger and
  the anchoring is already predicted and reconciled.
- **The dash is predicted**, because it is your own input and the delay is felt.

**So: nothing here needs changing today.** The one thing to add when sound lands
(item 5) is the same rule applied deliberately rather than by accident — see below.

**One refinement worth knowing about**, if an effect ever *is* predicted and turns
out wrong: the standard answer is not to remove it but to make the wrong version
cheap — a short flash that is already fading by the time a correction arrives is
forgivable in a way that a lingering scorch mark is not.

## 5. Sound effects

Nothing exists. **The largest single gap in feel**, and worth noting that it is
also the cheapest fix for two other items on this list: an explosion you can HEAR
tells you it happened even when it is off screen, and a rusher waking up is a
telegraph that currently only works if you are looking at it.

Design notes for whoever takes it:

- **The authority rule from item 4 applies unchanged.** A sound is even less
  retractable than a flash.
- **It is a spatial game with a fixed-yaw camera**, so positional audio
  (`AudioStreamPlayer3D`) carries real information — which side of you the
  explosion was on is gameplay.
- **Headless is the trap.** Everything in this project has to survive
  `--headless`, and an audio node that is only constructed in a real session is an
  audio node the gate never executes. Build it the way `blast_effect.gd` is
  built — really constructed in a test — and check the gate does not start
  printing driver warnings.
- The obvious first six: grenade throw, explosion, machine gun, rusher wake,
  pickup, and the downed/rescue moment.

## 6. Reduce the purple guy's accuracy — just slightly

The **skirmisher**. It fires the same round the player's machine gun does, through
the same `_spread` cone, so the knob already exists: `mg_spread_deg` (default 10°,
console-adjustable). But **that knob is shared with the player's gun**, so widening
it to nerf the skirmisher would nerf the player too.

The fix is therefore not a tuning pass, it is a **split**: an enemy spread that is
its own number. That is a small change and it is the right one — a skirmisher and
a player's weapon having independently tunable accuracy is something this game will
want repeatedly, and the alternative is a shared constant that can never be moved
without moving two things.

Do it as `SKIRMISHER_SPREAD_DEG`, defaulting slightly wider than the player's, with
its own console entry beside the existing one. Note the turret should probably
**not** inherit it — its threat is persistence, and a turret that also cannot aim
is a turret nobody respects.

## 7. Still no dorito for offscreen teammates, and no downed flash — **again**

Reported 2026-08-13 and **not built**. It is not blocked on anything and nothing
has been attempted; it simply has not been picked up while enemies and specials
were being built.

Worth restating why it matters more now than it did a round ago: the party has more
reasons to be spread out than it had yesterday — a shield anchors somebody in
place, a mine asks a player to hang back, and the two new enemies are answered by
closing and by taking cover respectively. **Every one of those pulls the group
apart**, which is exactly when knowing where a downed friend is stops being a
convenience.

The ask, unchanged: a **flashing indicator on a downed player**, and an **offscreen
marker** (the "dorito") pointing at teammates outside the view. The HUD already
knows the roster and the rescue state (`hud_model.gd`), so both are drawing
problems rather than plumbing ones.

## 8. A player can still get stuck in two inches of water — **again in spirit**

Water is **geometry only** — authored, built, and given no behaviour (see the
`playtest_bridge.seg` header, which says so). So "stuck in water" is a collision
result, not a water result: the body is catching on the *shape* of the water cell
or on the junction between it and the deck beside it.

That makes it a close relative of item 3, and the same discipline applies:
**measure the structure**. Specifically worth checking:

- Whether a water cell emits a deck slab at all, and at what height — the ramp
  paper-edge bug was exactly this shape of question, and `_build_deck` emits slabs
  for DECK and WATER only.
- The **junction** between a water cell and the deck beside it: two convex boxes
  meeting is where the ramp seam bug lived.
- Whether the body is stuck *horizontally* (caught on a lip) or *vertically*
  (sunk and unable to step out). Those have different causes and the report does
  not distinguish them.

Note this is the first report to survive the fix of a *different* geometry bug, so
it may share a root with the ramps. **If one measurement session is available,
measure both at once** — the deck builder is the common ancestor.

## What this list says as a whole

Three things are worth reading across the items rather than down them:

1. **The debug console paid for itself.** Item 1 is a tuning answer that would
   otherwise have cost a build, and items 2 and 3 both have a next step that
   starts with an existing instrument (`corrections`, `show_hitboxes`).
2. **Two items are repeats, and both are geometry.** Ramps and water are the same
   subsystem, and the ramp round already burned two attempts on rig artefacts
   before finding a real bug. The next attempt on either should start by measuring
   the built structure, not by walking a body over it.
3. **The gap is presentation, not mechanics.** Sound, the dorito and the downed
   flash are three of eight items, and none of them is blocked. M15 added a lot of
   things that happen; comparatively little was added that tells the player they
   happened.
