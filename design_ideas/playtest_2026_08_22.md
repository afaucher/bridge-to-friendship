## 1. Bullets should be slower — 10 feels right — and "increased lifetime"

**These are almost certainly one item, not two.** `MG_BULLET_LIFETIME` is not an
independent number: its comment says it is set "comfortably longer than range /
speed, so the RANGE is what stops a round and this is only the backstop for one
fired off the side of the bridge." Halve the speed and that stops being true.

| | at 22 m/s (shipped) | at 10 m/s |
|---|---|---|
| time to cross `MG_RANGE` (30 m) | 1.36 s | **3.0 s** |
| distance covered in `MG_BULLET_LIFETIME` (1.6 s) | 35 m — past range | **16 m** |

So at speed 10 the lifetime becomes the thing that stops a round, at a little
over **half the stated range**, and `MG_RANGE = 30` quietly becomes decorative.
That is what "increased lifetime" is: a consequence, not a second opinion. To
restore the original relationship it wants to be **~3.4 s**.

**`MG_BULLET_DROP` is the third number in the same knot, and it is not mentioned
in the report.** Its comment already records that this coupling bit once: *"drop
goes with the SQUARE of flight time, so the same fraction that gave 20 cm at
45 m/s gives five metres at 22"* — it was shrunk when the speed last fell. The
same arithmetic applies again:

- Drop acceleration is `MG_BULLET_DROP * GRAVITY` = 0.05 × 24 = 1.2 m/s².
- At 22 m/s over 30 m: **1.12 m** of drop, which matches the "about a metre" the
  comment claims.
- At 10 m/s over 30 m: **5.4 m**.

Five metres is a mortar, not a rifle. Holding the current feel means dividing the
constant by about five, to ~0.010. Whether that is *wanted* is a separate
question — a visible lob might be exactly right on a slow round — but it should
be a decision rather than something that arrives on its own.

**Two consequences worth deciding before this ships, neither of them raised:**

**Long range stops working against anything that moves.** At 10 m/s a round takes
3 s to cross 30 m, and `WALK_SPEED` is 6 — so a target simply walking sideways
covers 18 m during the flight. That may be the *point* of the request (a round is
something you watch cross the gap, and can be out from under), but it means the
30 m range is only real against a stationary target, and the change is much
larger than "bullets look better".

**Enemy fire slows down too.** Skirmishers and turrets fire the same object
through the same `_spawn_round`. Their rounds become far easier to walk out of —
on top of the 1–2 s alertness wake added 2026-08-21, which already gave the
player a window these enemies never had. The two changes stack in the same
direction, and this is the first session after the first of them.

**There is no debug knob for bullet speed**, so this number was presumably found
by editing the constant. If more sessions on it are expected, a
`mg_bullet_speed_pct` knob is the same shape as `rusher_speed_pct` — which
`playtest_2026_08_14.md` calls the clearest result of its day, precisely because
the knob let one session answer the question.

## 2. Players get snagged on walls — suggested a frictionless wall material

**The symptom is real and this project has already paid for its cause once. The
suggested fix cannot work.**

A frictionless `PhysicsMaterial` does nothing here: `CharacterBody3D.move_and_slide`
does not consult physics-material friction at all — that governs `RigidBody3D`.
A character slides along the collision *normals* it is given, so a wall that
returns a bad normal snags whatever its material says.

**The likely cause is a seam, and it is the exact bug the ramps were rebuilt to
avoid.** `SegmentBuilder._build_walls` emits **one collision box per cell, per
direction** — so a run of parapet along one edge is a row of separate boxes
abutting face to face, with a join every `CELL_SIZE`. The player is a
`CylinderShape3D`: flat-sided, no radius at the corner to roll over a join.

`_build_ramps` carries the note in as many words, from 2026-08-08:

> Per-cell wedges produce a seam at every cell boundary, and a flat-bottomed
> cylinder walking uphill catches on them: measured, a player climbed two cells
> of a four-cell ramp and then stopped dead, `on_wall`, with both reported
> contacts reading as clean floor. The surface was geometrically perfect — a
> raycast sweep showed an unbroken 26.6-degree slope — and the player still could
> not cross the join.

Ramps were merged into one prism per contiguous run for that reason. **Walls
never got the same treatment**, and nothing prompted it because sliding along a
parapet is a thing you do far less deliberately than climbing a ramp.

CLAUDE.md's companion note applies too: *where you cannot merge, OVERLAP — a gap
and a flush join are the same bug.* Two boxes placed exactly face to face are a
zero-width gap, and that is what a row of per-cell wall boxes is.

**So the shape of the fix is: merge co-planar wall runs into one box per run**,
the same as the ramp change, which also cuts the collision-shape count
substantially (`wall_box_count` is already tracked in `Built`, so the before and
after are measurable in one run). Overlapping neighbours slightly is the fallback
where a run genuinely cannot merge — a change in height along the run, for
instance.

**Worth confirming before building it**, because "snagged" has more than one
possible cause and this one is inferred rather than measured: walk a body along a
long parapet at full stick and record `get_slide_collision().get_depth()` and the
normals at each cell boundary. That also gives an A/B for the fix. It is the same
method that found the ramp seam, and CLAUDE.md is emphatic that when a bug is
intermittent, the question is what varies between the times it happens — here,
where along the wall you are.

## Speculative, same session

Not commitments — the list of what is worth trying next, with what is already
known about each.

**Rig for sounds — player and teammate sounds first.** There is no audio in this
project at all: no bus, no `AudioStreamPlayer`, no listener. `sound.md` ranks the
roster and puts **threat telegraphs (P0) above your own verbs**, on the argument
that a sound earns its slot by carrying information you cannot get another way.
**This session inverts that**: enemy sounds are explicitly low priority for now
and the player's own and teammates' sounds go first. That is a defensible
reordering — the first sound in a silent game is worth more for proving the rig
than for the information it carries, and *your own gun* is the one event you can
trigger at will while building — but it is a reordering, and `sound.md` should
not be left quietly disagreeing with what got built. A first placeholder is being
prepared: a recorded "pew" for the machine gun.

**An alternate laser mode that draws a DOT at the aim point.** This picks the M21
thread back up. That plan was parked 2026-08-21 with the note that the laser
sight was not staying, which killed its preferred readout, and that the two
remaining holes — "where am I aiming" and "where will this land" — looked like
one world-space marker. A dot at the aim point *is* that marker. It also answers
the pad-aim gap that removing the beam reopened: `AimSource.PAD_CURSOR_RANGE`
projects 6 m along facing and is currently invisible.

**A charge bar near the aim point, below it.** The other half of the same idea,
and it is the better version of what M21 proposed. That plan put the charge
readout in the HUD corner, and the parking note already recorded the objection: a
player throwing a grenade is looking at the *world*, not at the corner of the
screen. Putting it under the aim dot puts it where the eye already is. Note the
existing slot bar currently draws zero for a grenade — a grenade never touches
`fire_timer` — so the wiring is small.

**Bullet impact indicators — particles?** Worth building, and the timing is not a
coincidence: rounds just got slow enough to watch, which makes the moment they
*land* legible for the first time. `sound.md` already argues the same point from
the audio side (P0 #8, the near-miss whiz-by: a round that misses is survivable
information).

Two things make this cheap. **There is exactly one place to hook it** —
`_resolve_round_hit` in `game_world.gd` is where every round in the game resolves,
whoever fired it. And **there is precedent for the shape**: `scripts/ui/blast_effect.gd`
already exists for the rocket, so an impact effect is a second customer for a
pattern rather than a new subsystem.

The open question is what it should *say*. A generic spark reads as "something
happened here"; a hit that distinguishes **deck from body from hat from parapet**
tells the shooter whether they connected, which at 10 m/s and 10° of spread is
genuinely unclear right now. That is the version worth building, and it is the
same information a hit-marker gives in other games without needing one on the HUD.
