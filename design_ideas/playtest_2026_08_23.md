work can be scoped rather than guessed at.

Sorted by *kind of work* rather than by the order reported: one done, two bugs, two

## Done

### 0. The hold music is mono *(fixed 2026-08-23)*

Suspected to be marked stereo while holding one channel of audio. Measured, that
is nearly right, and the detail decided the fix: the channels are **not**
identical and neither is silent — correlation 0.94, with LEFT being RIGHT at
−10.3 dB and 12% residual. One signal captured with a quiet copy of itself.

Collapsed to the **right channel alone** rather than averaged. Averaging is what
Godot's `force/mono=true` does, and summing a decorrelated copy 10 dB down is comb
filtering rather than a downmix — it would have coloured the track and cost 3.7 dB
against simply taking the signal. Source 8.95 MB → 4.48; shipped zip 40.1 → 39.3.

**Noted and deliberately not acted on:** the track is quiet (peak −12.6 dBFS, RMS
−36.5) and `LobbyMusic.FULL_DB` then plays it 10 dB further down again. That is a
level decision rather than a format one, and changing two things at once is how
you cannot tell which one helped.

## Bugs

### 1. The closing countdown is frozen at 30 s for everyone but the host

**Confirmed by reading, and the cause is one missing line.**

`close_timer` counts down inside `RoundMachine._step_closing`, and
`round_machine.step()` is called from `_host_tick` only. A client's copy is
written by `_round_sync`, which fires **on state change** — so a client receives
`30.0` at the moment CLOSING begins and nothing ever decrements it. `hud_model.gd`
reads `machine.close_timer` and faithfully shows the stale number.

**The intended design is written down and was never built.** `_on_round_state_changed`
says:

> The countdown is re-sent with it and again on the snapshot interval; a client
> ticking its own copy down between those is right to within a frame, and the
> frame it is wrong by is a number on a screen rather than anything the sim reads.

So the fix is the client ticking its own copy, exactly as that comment describes.

**Check its twin before fixing.** `round_clock` accumulates in the same two
functions and is shown on the HUD the same way. If the countdown is frozen on a
client, the round timer almost certainly is too, and nobody has reported it
because a clock counting UP from zero looks plausible while it is wrong.

### 2. Networking is still wrong, and it feels WORSE OVER TIME

**No diagnosis here on purpose.** The ask was explicit: *"Lets not guess at what
is happening. If we could instrument and then look at the logs to see it would be
a better way to spot the actual issue."* That is also this project's own rule —
*prefer a DIRECT COUNT at the line that does the thing; eliminating candidates by
reading can be argued with, and often wrongly.* So what follows is a list of
instruments, not a list of suspects.

**"Worse over time" is the most useful thing in the report,** because it is a
SHAPE. Whatever is wrong accumulates, and an instrument that samples once tells
you nothing about it. Every number below therefore wants a **periodic row in a
file** — one line a second, fixed columns, host and client both — so the trend is
readable afterwards rather than watched live.

**What already exists** and should be reused rather than rebuilt:

| | |
|---|---|
| `GameWorld.corrections` | count of prediction corrections |
| `correction_metres` | total distance corrected — the volume, not just the count |
| `correction_worst` | the largest single correction |
| `debug_inbound_delay_ticks` | artificial latency injection, already used by `test_contact_prediction` |
| `net_log` | a debug knob for per-event connect/spawn/sync prints |
| `SNAPSHOT_INTERVAL_TICKS` | currently **1** — a snapshot every tick |

Three counters and a latency injector is a real head start; what is missing is
that none of it is **sampled over time or written anywhere**.

**What to add, in the order that a "gets worse" signal would be narrowed:**

1. **Snapshot SIZE, per section, per second.** `SnapshotDelta.encode` already
   splits by section (`players`, `bullets`, `hats`, `gunners`, `zombies`…), so the
   columns come for free — and if the total climbs, the section that climbs names
   the pool. This is the single most likely instrument to answer the question in
   one session.
2. **Entity counts per pool, per second.** The direct version of the same
   question, and it distinguishes "more objects" from "more bytes per object".
3. **Corrections per second, and metres per second**, rather than the running
   totals — a total always rises, so it cannot show a *rate* getting worse. This
   is the max/duty-cycle distinction CLAUDE.md already records.
4. **Client tick minus host tick**, sampled. The clock has been wrong before
   (2026-08-18, a client counting its own ticks) and it is cheap to watch.
5. **Snapshots RECEIVED per second on the client**, counted at the line that
   consumes one — never at the send. *"Attempted is not delivered"* is in
   CLAUDE.md because this project has already shipped a counter that measured
   sends.

**Two rules the log itself has to follow**, both learned here:

- **The heartbeat must be unconditional.** A row every second on a path no game
  state can gate, or you cannot tell hung from slow — and a row that stops is
  itself the finding.
- **The file must be per-run and stamped.** *"A DURABLE log is guilty until proven
  fresh"*; reading a stale one has already cost this project a wrong call about a
  red gate.

**And the instrument needs validating against a case that must fail**, which is
the one piece of luck here: `debug_inbound_delay_ticks` already exists, so the
telemetry can be checked by injecting 40 ticks of delay and confirming the numbers
move. An instrument that has never reported a fault is not evidence.

#### BUILT 2026-08-23 — `scripts/net/net_telemetry.gd`

All five columns above, one row a second, per-run and stamped. The validation
earned its keep immediately: the first column tried was `correction_metres` — the
client's error about **itself** — which reported **0.00 m at 4 ticks of injected
delay and 0.00 m at 40**, because a client replays its own inputs and lands in the
same place however stale its news is. That is the "probe that could only ever
return zero" this project has now twice believed. The column that can see it is
`jump` / `jump_max`: how far a *remote* body was teleported by an arriving
snapshot. Be honest about what that measures — **discontinuity, not lag.** A
client steadily half a second behind still receives one tick of movement per
snapshot, so a laggy session with `jump` flat has narrowed the search, not come up
empty.

#### THE SURFACING DECISION, 2026-08-23 — collect on the host

The two sides see **disjoint** things, which is a fact about where the counters
are incremented rather than a preference:

| | counted at | on the other side |
|---|---|---|
| `corr*` | the client's reconciliation | structurally **0** on the host |
| `jump*` | the client's snapshot-apply | structurally **0** on the host |
| `b_*`, `memo` | the host's encoder | structurally **0** on the client |
| `snaps` | both — **different meanings** | host = attempted, client = arrived |

So neither file is a superset, and that last row is the sharp one: a host's
`snaps` is a send count, and reading it as delivery is the exact counter this
project has already shipped once and misread.

The split maps onto the question: **the symptom is only observable on a client**
(a host has none by construction — it *is* the truth), while **the leading cause,
payload growth, is only measurable on the host.**

They are joinable on `tick`, which is a shared key only because of the 2026-08-18
clock fix — `t` is not, since the files open at different wall-clock moments. But
the decision was **collection, not joining, and for logistics rather than data**:
three people play, and asking two of them to go and find `user://` afterwards is a
step that does not survive contact with a Tuesday night. Each client now sends its
finished row to the host once a second and the host files it under `from`, so one
reachable machine holds every machine's numbers, one row per (second, source).

Sent **unreliably on purpose**: a degrading session is exactly when a report will
be lost, and a *missing row* says so where a retried one would paper over it. Read
a gap as missing, never as zero — the file makes that legible by having no row
rather than a row of noughts. Clients keep writing their own file too, as the
control if the reporting channel itself is ever suspect.

#### The marker key — F4

The one column that comes from a human. *"Worse over time"* is a feeling until it
has a timestamp against it, and no additional counter closes that gap. Any player
may press it, including a client: the press rides that client's next row up, so it
lands in the same row as the numbers taken at that moment, and **the person with
the symptom is the one who gets to point at it.** Counted rather than flagged —
somebody hammering it is telling you something a boolean would flatten.

**Not built, deliberately: a live on-screen overlay.** It shows a number at the
moment you are least able to interpret it, and the marker key buys the same
correlation for less.

**Still open: nobody has read a real session's trace.** That needs a session with
the symptom in it, and it is the next step on this item.

## Cheap

### 4. Hat stack HEIGHT breaks a tie on hat COUNT

`RoundMachine.rank_entries` sorts on `hats` (a count), then `made_it`, then peer
id. This inserts a criterion between the first two.

It is a real distinction rather than a restatement, and only because of the
merchant: `HatStyle.slot_height` gives a trophy `TALL_HAT_SLOTS` (3.5) against an
ordinary hat's 1. So three hats including a trophy stands visibly taller than
three ordinary ones, and this makes the board agree with what the players can see.

`rank_entries` is a pure function already tested as a table of cases, and
`display_ranks` derives the printed rank from equality on the same fields — so
**both must learn the new criterion together**, or two players with equal counts
and different heights are ordered correctly and both printed "1st".

### 5. Four sillier metrics

`StatRegistry` says how: *"append ONE entry to STATS and put ONE `_bump` at the
line where..."*. So each is one registry entry and one call site.

- **Most backwards steps**
- **Most hats lost**
- **Most altitude gained**
- **Most shots while walking**

**"Most altitude gained" is currently meaningless and becomes meaningful under
item 8.** The bridge is pitched 4°, so walking forward gains altitude for free and
the metric would rank whoever travelled furthest. Flatten the bridge and it
measures what it says: ramps, towers and lifts. Worth building *after* the untilt,
or it ships measuring the wrong thing.

**"Most shots while walking" needs its predicate stated.** Walking is not a state
the way `SHOVE` or `CLIMB` are — it is the absence of the others plus a non-zero
input. Decide whether it means "moving" or "not standing still", because a player
shuffling on the spot will otherwise farm it.

## Milestones

### 6. Call for help

A player signals distress, on three channels at once:

- **On screen** — flash their health bar bigger
- **Off screen** — flash their teammate marker (the dorito) bigger
- **Audio**

All three surfaces exist: `hud.gd` owns the bars, `teammate_markers.gd` owns the
doritos, and `shot_sound.gd` is the pattern for a positional one-shot.

**The audio is prepared and imported.** `sounds/call_for_help.wav` — 635 ms, mono
44.1 kHz, peak −3 dBFS, trimmed from a 1.88 s take. The source held two syllables
of one utterance (580–780 ms and 810–1150 ms) plus the recorder's stop click at
1800 ms; both syllables are kept and the click is gone.

**Note what it is NOT positional about.** A gunshot is a thing that happened
somewhere. A call for help is a thing that happened *to somebody*, and the player
it is about may be off screen — which is the entire point. Whether it attenuates
with distance is a design decision, not a default: a cry you cannot hear from the
far end of the bridge fails at the one distance it exists for.

**Open: what stops it being spam.** Nothing about the three channels answers how
often it may be pressed.

**DONE 2026-08-23** — `ACTION_CALL` on Q / gamepad Y, a 2.5 s call on a 5 s
cooldown, all three channels on the shared `CrisisFlash` clock so a player in
trouble does one thing rather than three things at three frequencies. The
cooldown is the answer to the spam question: holding the button gives one call.
Audio at 120 m, which is twice the camera's framing — a cry you cannot hear from
the far end of the bridge fails at the one distance it exists for, so it is
positional for *direction* and effectively not for *audibility*.

**And it is not only a key.** Going DOWNED calls by itself, and the cooldown is
CLEARED rather than consulted at that moment: a player who called two seconds
before being tumbled is the likeliest caller there is — they already knew they
were in trouble — and under a consulted cooldown they would be the one player
whose collapse is silent. It then re-issues on the ordinary cooldown for as long
as they are down: three cries over a fifteen-second bleed-out, with gaps, because
2.5 s of call against 15 s of bleeding out is loud for a sixth of the timer and
silent for the part where somebody is deciding whether to come — while a
continuous one is an alarm nobody can locate.

The call is still available in *every* state, deliberately, including LEDGE_HANG:
the states with no other verb are the ones the feature is for.

### 7. A skill game to climb back up or revive yourself

A window marked on the health bar; when the moving value passes it, press **E**
(use) and you climb back up. Pressing E outside the window **skips the bar ahead**
— so a mistimed press costs you position and makes the next chance worse.

The trade-off, as described: *do I take the chance, or make my revive odds worse?*

**THIS REVERSES A WRITTEN DECISION, and it should be reversed deliberately rather
than by accident.** `game_world.gd` says, at the wipe rule:

> NO SPECIAL CASE FOR A PARTY OF ONE. A solo hang runs its timer, drops, and then
> wipes, exactly like any other — the grab delays the restart instead of causing
> it. **A lone player still has no way out of a hang, which is by design: hanging
> is a co-op state and solo is a practice mode.**

A self-revive makes solo survivable, which is a good thing to want. But it also
changes what a teammate rescue is FOR. The design that keeps both is a
self-revive that is *possible but slow and risky* against a teammate haul that is
*fast and certain* — so the skill game is what you do when nobody is coming, not
the better option.

The relevant numbers today: `LEDGE_HANG_SECONDS` 8.0, `DOWNED_SECONDS` 15.0,
`REVIVE_SECONDS` and `LEDGE_HAUL_SECONDS` for the assisted versions, and
`rescue_progress` is already a replicated 0..1 the HUD can draw.

**Open, and each changes the build:**

- **Which bar is moving?** `rescue_progress`, health, or a dedicated sweep? "When
  the current health passes it" reads like a value rising toward full.
- **What happens if it completes with no successful press?** The hang timer
  expiring is currently a fall.
- **Does it apply to both `LEDGE_HANG` and `DOWNED`,** or only the hang?
- **How does a teammate arriving mid-attempt interact with it?**

## The big one

### 8. Aiming: target what is aimable, otherwise level — and untilt the bridge

The feedback was split, and the path forward:

- When the cursor is on a **targetable thing**, aim there.
- Otherwise aim **horizontally**.
  - Open: at the player's own height, or at the height the cursor is over?
  - The driving comment: *"don't usually want to shoot at the ground."*
- To make "horizontal" a stable definition, **untilt the bridge** and give plinko
  balls artificial gravity instead.

#### Most of the aiming half already exists as two knobs

M20 built three switches deliberately, *"because an A/B with one lever cannot say
which half worked"*. What is being described is close to `aim_mode = level` plus
`aim_assist = snap`:

- `level` aims at a point 30 m down the bearing **at the shooter's own height**,
  so a shot always leaves flat. That is the answer to "don't shoot at the ground",
  and it is what shipped before `point` became the default on 2026-08-20.
- `snap` pulls the aim onto an enemy's centre of mass when the shot would already
  pass close to one.

**IT WAS TRIED AS THE DEFAULT ON 2026-08-23 AND THE GATE REFUSED IT.** That is
the useful outcome, and it is worth reading before anyone tries it again.

`test_tower_enemy` measured the case: M23's watchpost turret sits **2.47 m above
the muzzle at 7.17 m**, a level shot's closest approach is **2.54 m**, and
`AIM_SNAP_RADIUS` is **0.6**. The assist is four times too small to reach a tower,
so level+snap ships the hazard-with-no-counter that put `point` in in the first
place. The test says so in its own words: *"under `level` this is 0 by
construction and the round passes under the post forever, which is why phase 0 had
to land before a tower could carry anything."*

**THE DIFFERENCE IS THE WORD "CURSOR", and my earlier reading of this was too
optimistic.** The request is *aim there when the cursor is ON a targetable thing*.
`snap` works off the **ray** — it pulls shots that would already pass close to
something. Those two agree everywhere except the one case that matters: a target
your cursor is resting on and your level ray is metres beneath.

So what was asked for is a **third mode**, not a combination of the two that
exist: point-aim when the cursor rests on something aimable, level otherwise. That
is a small piece of code — `aim_target` already has both answers in it and needs a
predicate to choose between them — and it has no tower hole, because putting the
cursor on the turret is exactly the case it handles.

It also makes "very carefully choose what is aimable" the whole design rather than
a footnote: under this mode that list is not an assist, it is the difference
between a shot going where you pointed and going flat. The open question about height is answered in passing: `level` picks the
shooter's own, which is what "don't shoot at the ground" asks for, so the third
mode should do the same when it falls back.

#### "Very carefully choose what is aimable" — the current answer is narrow

`_snap_to_enemy` considers `_rushers`, `_gunners` and `_zombies`, within
`AIM_SNAP_RADIUS`, and only *in front of* the shooter and *nearer* than the point
asked for — with a deliberately tight radius, because *"pointing at the ground near
something is a real strategy for an area weapon against a slow target."*

Everything else in the world is currently not aimable: hats on heads, plinko
balls, stones, shooters' pillars, mounds, graves, deployables, other players. Each
is a decision, and the two that most want deciding are **worn hats** (they are
already a shootable target with their own layer) and **other players** (friendly
fire is on).

#### Untilting: one line, and it removes more than it breaks

`BRIDGE_PITCH_DEG` is applied in exactly one place — `bridge_grid.gd:328`,
`rotation.x = deg_to_rad(...)`. Setting it to zero flattens everything downstream.

**Its stated purpose is plinko and only plinko:**

> THE WHOLE BRIDGE IS TILTED, so that "up the bridge" is genuinely uphill. This is
> what makes plinko work. A shooter drops spheres and they must come DOWN the
> bridge at the players under their own weight [...] and it costs one rotation on
> the grid root rather than a rule anywhere in the sim.

**And it has since become a problem that two other systems work around:**

- `special_body.gd` — *"A DROPPED WEAPON MUST NOT ROLL. The deck is pitched 4
  degrees by design, and a body that keeps rolling never drops below
  `SPECIAL_SETTLE_SPEED`, so it never becomes LOOSE and is never collectable — a
  gun that runs away down the bridge."*
- `hat_body.gd` — a hat that lands on its side rolls down the pitch, and its
  rotation is locked to stop it.

So the trade is: **replace one deliberate mechanism with an explicit one, and
retire two workarounds.** The balls get a constant force down-bridge, which is
what the pitch was giving them implicitly, applied to the one object that wanted
it rather than to the whole world.

**It also retires a class of bug.** CLAUDE.md's ladder-face entry is exactly this:
*"the art compared grid-LOCAL surface heights and the climb compared WORLD ones,
on a bridge pitched 4 degrees."* With the pitch at zero, local and world Y agree,
and every "compare heights across different Z" trap goes with it.

**What to watch:** the pitch is also atmosphere — it is what makes up-bridge read
as uphill. Flat may read as a corridor rather than a climb. That is a look
question and only a playtest answers it, so it is worth flipping the constant and
walking around before doing any of the work that depends on it.

**And it interacts with M25.** A flat bridge makes "horizontal" stable, which is
the stated reason — but it is also what a sidescrolling shooter corridor wants,
and what a bus route wants. Worth doing before the minigame work rather than
after.

## Hats: the question about the seed

> *There is a question I think for hats — the seed is what gets recorded but that
> isn't enough to preserve the state?*

**The recorded number IS enough — for one hat.** `HatConfig` saves a style id, and
`HatStyle.knobs(style_id)` is a pure function of it: base, top, rim, height, curl
and colour all fall out of the same integer. The file says so directly: *"the same
saved value is the same hat forever and on every machine."* Nothing about a hat's
appearance is lost.

**Two things are lost, and neither is fidelity:**

1. **Only one id is stored.** `_persistable_top` picks the topmost hat and saves
   that. A stack cannot be represented, so "save our whole hat stack" is a change
   from an `int` to an array — the format, `_remember_hat`'s equality check, and
   the restore path.
2. **A trophy is deliberately refused.** `_persistable_top` skips tall hats, and
   the reasoning is recorded at length (2026-08-18): *"trading away your only hat
   really does cost you your saved hat — which is the same rule as losing it to a
   fall, and the reason the trade is a bet rather than a freebie."*

**So both hat asks reverse decisions with stated reasons, and that is fine as long
as it is on purpose.** Saving big hats makes the merchant trade a purchase rather
than a bet. Saving the whole stack makes a fall cost you nothing across sessions.
Both weaken the same thing — that hats are held at risk — and the interesting
version is probably partial: keep the trophy, or keep the stack, but not both, and
possibly only on a run you *finished*.
